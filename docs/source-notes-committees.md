# Source notes: what each committee handles, and what the committee roles mean

Written Oct 5, 2026 for phase 1 of `docs/plan-explain-committees-and-seats.md`. Every source below was read by script on Oct 5, 2026
(`scripts/fetch_explainers.py`, which `python scripts/refresh.py --explainers` runs) and the passages were read by eye. Nothing here marks
a source as cleared: each is registered in `scripts/us_sources.py` as `review_required` until a person reads its terms and signs below,
the same way as `docs/source-terms-review.md`. Which passage of which page is in `scripts/us_explainer_config.py`.

Result: all 49 committees and 171 of the 181 subcommittees have official text in `data/us-explainers-2026.json`; 10 subcommittees say
"No description on file" (listed at the end). Two runs of the fetcher on the live sources wrote the identical file
(SHA-256 `6ce66900c0620c12dea50271d559e55137a23685b255fdaf938d872e99f8b751`). The pulled dates in the data are days in UTC, the convention of the other federal records, so pages read
on the evening of Oct 5, 2026 (Eastern) are stamped Oct 6.

## Not usable

- **Congress.gov committee pages and CRS reports** (congress.gov, crsreports.congress.gov): every script request was answered HTTP 403 on Oct 5. Registered as `us_congress_gov_committees`, status `blocked`.
- **The Senate glossary** (senate.gov/reference/glossary_term/...): every term page now answers with the Senate's 404 page.
- **The Government Manual** (`us_gov_manual`): not needed; the chamber rules and the committees' own pages cover every committee.
- **uscode.house.gov** (maintenance page) and **jct.gov** (403): the govinfo.gov edition of the Code is used instead.

## Committees read against their sources

All 49 committee rows were read against their source by eye, among them: House Agriculture, Appropriations, Ethics (one subject, "The Code of Official Conduct", so Rule XI clause 3(a)(2) is shown with it), Rules, Ways and Means, and Intelligence; Senate Finance, Foreign Relations, Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs, Indian Affairs, Ethics, and Aging; and the Joint Economic Committee, the Joint Committee on Taxation, the Library and Printing committees, and the Helsinki Commission.

## Things a person should look at

1. **Who chairs the Helsinki Commission.** csce.gov's Leadership page says "The current Chairman is Senator Roger Wicker"; the congress-legislators record lists him as Cochairman. The app shows the record's word and does not choose.
2. **Agriculture subcommittees.** The House Agriculture subcommittees page and its adopted rules (Rule XI(c), Jan 23, 2025) assign horticulture, bees, and organics differently; the fetcher reads the rules.
3. **Pages with a Congress in the address** (Foreign Affairs) or a printed date (the Oversight and Financial Services committee rules on govinfo.gov) will need new addresses in January 2027.
4. **Official text with typing errors** (Foreign Affairs: "Near EasternAffairs", "alignwith") is kept as published.
5. **Dashes.** The app prints no em or en dash, so in official text an em dash is written as two hyphens and an en dash as one, the way GPO prints them in plain text. The words are unchanged.
6. **Senate Appropriations, Labor, HHS, and Education** (SSAP18): the agency list is long; the passage ends at the department lines.
7. **Some Transportation and Infrastructure pages** mix the committee's plans ("During the 119th Congress the Committee will work to...") into their jurisdiction section. The plain lines do not use those sentences.
